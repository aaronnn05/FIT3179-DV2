1Who is looking up?

Anyone can rate their night sky from 1 (bright) to 7 (very dark) on the Globe at Night website.

Most ratings come from the big east-coast cities.

One night, 21 June 2020, brought in 4,425 of the 6,432 reports. A national event asked Australians to rate their sky on the longest night of the year.

Counted per million people, so big and small states compare fairly, the order changes.

The Australian Capital Territory comes first with 537 reports per million people, ahead of the Northern Territory and Queensland.

But look at the survey night.

The Australian Capital Territory's lead comes from that one night: 464 per million then, only 74 on all other nights. On other nights, the Northern Territory and Queensland report the most.

2Where is the sky darkest?

Not near the big cities.

Melbourne scores 2, Sydney and Perth 3. The darkest squares score 6, inland and in the far south.

Most people rate their sky somewhere in the middle.

City lights hide faint stars, so a truly dark sky is rare: just 3 in every 100 reports.

3Where do people meet?

Most astronomy clubs are near the big east-coast cities.

Clubs follow people, so most of the country has none nearby.

New South Wales has 17 clubs.

Western Australia, the largest state by area, has 2.

Per million people, the Northern Territory comes first.

It has only 1 club, but so few people that one is enough to top the list. Treat this lead with care. South Australia and Western Australia come last.

4What gets people outside?

Of four big sky events, aurora gets the most searches in every state.

Tasmania, the best place in Australia to see one, searches for little else: 91% of its searches are about aurora.

Share of the state's searches (%) under 5 5 to 10 10 to 15 15 to 20 20 to 30 30 to 60 over 60

Interest stays low until the Sun acts up.

A huge solar storm in May 2024 sent searches to their highest point in five years.

Google scores searches from 0 to 100, where 100 is the busiest week.

5Do you need a telescope?

No one publishes telescope sales, so we used Google searches instead.

They hardly move. The darker months line up with sky news: Jupiter and Saturn meeting in December 2020, then the James Webb Space Telescope's launch in December 2021 and its first pictures in July 2022, the busiest month of all.

Searches for gear and photography stayed flat.

The growth is in plain "stargazing", from 7 to 20: people who just want to look.

Earlier spikes lasted a month. This rise has lasted more than two years.

6So, should you look up?

Yes, and you don't need much to start.

  • 4,425 of 6,432 sky reports came from one survey night, so a single event can bring thousands outside.
  • The darkest skies score 6 out of 7, far from Melbourne, Sydney and Perth.
  • Most of the 45 clubs sit on the east coast. Elsewhere, you may be on your own.
  • Searches for plain "stargazing" rose from 7 to 20, while gear and photography stayed flat.

Interest is growing among people who just want to look, not buy gear. If you live outside the east coast, there may be no club nearby, so look for the darkest sky you can reach and start there.

Data sources

  • Globe at Night: sky ratings in Australia, 2017 to 2025. Invalid scores and locations removed.
  • IceInSpace: list of astronomy clubs. Club locations are approximate town centres.
  • Google Trends: searches in Australia and by state for telescope, stargazing and aurora, downloaded October 2026.
  • Australian Bureau of Statistics: state populations, March 2026.
  • Natural Earth: state boundaries.

Notes

  • Globe at Night ratings come from volunteers, so they show who took part, not everyone who looks up. The 21 June 2020 spike came from a light-pollution mapping event run by the Australasian Dark Sky Alliance.
  • Google Trends scores are relative (100 = busiest point), not counts. Searches are not sales.
  • The waffle chart rounds the 6,432 reports to 100 squares (file: data/sky_score_waffle.csv).
  • Built with Vega-Lite and pure.css, hosted on GitHub Pages.
  • AI use: I used Claude (Anthropic) to help plan the story, clean the data and write the chart and page code. I checked the results against the sources.
